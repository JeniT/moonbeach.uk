There was a time for perhaps 20 years where every time I got a new PC or laptop, among the first things I would install on it would be the original Neverwinter Nights. I played the original campaign from start to finish twice times in single player, and the first Chapter I-don’t-know-how-many-times in multiplayer. Then there were the official add-ons, with 10’s of hours more play, and the unofficial but fully supported 3rd party adventures, with 100’s of hours more. And then there was the Aurora toolset, the very thing the dev team had used to build the game, and which enabled all those module-makers to unleash their creativity. I’m sure I spent as many hours in the toolset building my own adventures as I did actually playing the game. The last thing I built was called simply 'The Quest', and it became my first adventure written for publication, with a rebranding as 'The Eldritch Earl', which will be live any day now honest, and I will be posting more about in the near future. 

Anyway, when I saw the sadly all-too-predictable news about Bioware’s closure the other week, so many happy memories came rushing back, not only of Neverwinter and its player-empowerment, but also all the other incredible games they gave us over the years: *Knights of the Old Republic*, *Jade Empire*, *Mass Effect*, and *Dragon Age* being ones I personally loved. It’s an incredible track record, and while it’s obviously not terrible news for all the folks at Bioware, they have left an unsurpassed legacy of CRPGs to be proud of.
